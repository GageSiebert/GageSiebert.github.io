I'm a PhD candidate at Arizona State University, working with Sara Walker on applied algorithmic complexity. My thesis involves formalizing interpretable, substrate-specific languages of computation, building software that makes complexity-theoretic analyses practical, and using these tools to find hierarchical structure in biological systems.

After my PhD I want to work on AI safety, particularly intepretability. I think this is among the most important problems to work on right now. Much of my PhD research has addressed the question: given a system, how can we build a computational model of it that is interpretable. I've spent my PhD building formal tools to answer that for chemical and biological systems, and I'd like to bring the same approach to neural networks.

Before ASU, I studied physics and math at UW–Madison, where I worked on radio cosmology, millisecond pulsars, the optics of a NASA balloon microwave telescope, and the origin of life.
