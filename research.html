Research

My PhD work, with Sara Walker at ASU, is on applied algorithmic complexity. Measures like Kolmogorov complexity depend on a choice of reference machine, the language in which a computation is described. I work on making that choice principled: formalizing interpretable, substrate-specific languages of computation, developing assembly spaces as a basis for physical complexity, and building software that makes these analyses usable. I apply these methods to identify and characterize hierarchical structure in biological systems.

Earlier, I worked on observational astrophysics and instrumentation: a search in LIGO data for signatures of a physics without the continuum (testing the Born rule), NASA's EXCLAIM balloon telescope, the Tianlai radio array, and millisecond pulsars at Arecibo.
